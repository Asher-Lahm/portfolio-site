Computer Friends

About Computer Friends

Computer Friends is an interactive art piece that generates collections of shapes called "friends"!

The idea behind this project was to create a whimsical creature generator that creates unique digital beings, each with distinct attributes and personality.

I was inspired by digital pets from games like Wobbledogs, where the random traits of digital dogs makes each one feel uniquely special and endearing. Another inspiration was Cary Huang's evolution simulations on Youtube, where abstract simulated creatures seemingly develop emergent storylines and personalities.

I found it fascinating how storylines and genuine emotional connections could emerge from entities composed of just data and simple rules. With Computer Friends, I wanted to explore that same idea, and make people feel empathy for something as abstract and data-driven as a collection of shapes and patterns!

A major visual inspiration for the project were barn quilts. These are traditional American quilts displayed on barns, and feature vibrant colors and bold geometric designs. I've always loved the distinct geometric look of quilts, and it's a medium I'd love to explore further some day.

I began development reusing code I had developed to generate shapes in a grid. I had made it expandable and modular for easy integration into future projects.

I created a simple generator that made entirely random patterns. However, pure randomness often didn't look very appealing and had very little character. The next step would be to refine the randomness.

I decided that these friends needed some kind of focus point, almost like a face! I gave each friend a simple and bold main pattern that sits on top. This gives the viewer something to focus on. I then added some random chances for certain modifications. Some patterns might be all circles or all squares. Some may be dense, sparse, or even striped! These adjustments gave each friends far more personality. They all still felt random, but not uniform.

This project was displayed at the December 2025 Open Studios at the Johnny Carson Center for Emerging Media Arts. I observed as people generated new Computer Friends and reacted to each one! Some were mesmerized by certain color schemes, while others found the ones with chaotic movements and odd shapes to be funny. The mix of reactions was just what I had wanted to hear. It brought me so much joy to see others interacting with my work!
